# Review, 17 Nov 2026

Asked for by Faisal before the tower session: where the game is, how to get
to something that is enjoyable to play, good to look at and satisfying, and
what to watch out for. A snapshot, not a rulebook. Proposals here are
suggestions until Faisal says yes; the ones he accepts move to the roadmap.

## In one paragraph

**The look is ahead of the play.** Every version since v29 went to how the
game looks or how its buttons work. None went to how it plays, and nobody
has measured that. The map, the Sprout and the level 1 towers now look like
one game. Whether a run is fun, fair and worth repeating is still an open
question, and it is the one that decides whether the art was worth making.
The plan is sound. The risk is spending weeks polishing towers for a game
that has not been checked for fun.

## What a quick playthrough showed

A script played the campaign with fixed, simple ways of spending (17 Nov,
v34.1 rules). These are crude players: they place by trail coverage and
cannot react to what a wave contains. A person will do better. Read it as a
floor, not a verdict.

| Way of playing | Map 1 | Map 2 |
|---|---|---|
| No towers | Lost on wave 2 | |
| Three Gnomes, never upgrade | Lost on wave 5 (the boss) | |
| Only place towers, never upgrade (7 towers) | 8 of 20 lives left | Lost on wave 3 |
| One of each tower, then cheapest upgrades | 9 of 20 lives left | Lost on wave 5 |
| Two towers, everything into their levels | 4 of 20 lives left | Lost on wave 2 |

Two things follow:

1. **The brief says the maps are too easy. That is not established.** It
   was inferred from trail coverage, not from playing. None of these
   players reached map 3. The truth for a person is unknown until someone
   writes down how their runs go.
2. **A rough first map dooms the second.** Lives carry over, and the coins
   you start the next map with are paid per life kept. Lose lives and you
   arrive both weaker and poorer. Every one of these players lost 3 lives
   on the very first wave. For a new player that is a spiral, not a
   challenge.

## Suggestions, in the order I would do them

1. **Play three full runs now, before the tower session,** and fill in
   `docs/playtest-notes.md` after each. Ten minutes a run, no code. It is
   the cheapest information in the project and it may reorder everything
   after it.
2. **While you are in the tower session, I build the balance simulator**
   (`tools/sim.js`, already planned for later). It touches no game file, so
   it does not wait in the queue. Then "is it too hard" has a number.
3. **After the tower tryout, one version for information and feedback**
   before any tower is drawn at 7 stages: the next wave shown beside GO,
   bug traits shown (armored, cannot be slowed, boss), a visible burst when
   a tower levels up, clearer hits and deaths. These change how a run feels
   more than any sprite, and they are small.
4. **Bugs before the full tower stages,** or at least alongside the first
   tower. You watch bugs for most of a run. Four beautiful towers shooting
   at five rough bugs will still look unfinished.
5. **Soften the spiral,** once the simulator can compare options: a floor
   on the coins you arrive with, a few lives back between maps, or lives
   reset per map with a score kept instead. Pick by numbers, then by feel.
6. **Maps 2 to 4 stay frozen until five other people have finished the
   map 1 slice.** You will be too good at your own game to judge it.

## Best practices

- **Play it, do not look at it.** A screenshot answers "is it pretty". Only
  a run on the phone answers "is it fun". Every decision that mattered so
  far was settled in the game, not in a mock.
- **One change, then compare.** When two things change at once you cannot
  tell which one helped. Keep the previous preview open beside the new one.
- **Know what you are judging.** Before each preview, say the one question
  it answers. If you cannot, it is not ready to be judged.
- **Readability first.** In a tower defense the order of loudness is: bugs,
  shots, towers, the Sprout, the ground. If a new tower is the loudest
  thing on screen it is wrong, however good it looks alone.
- **Every action gets an answer.** Place, shoot, hit, kill, upgrade, fork,
  a bug getting through, a wave cleared: each should have something you
  see and something you hear. Go down that list when the game feels flat.
- **Sound is half of "satisfying".** It is parked. Do not leave it to the
  very end: better beeps for hit, kill and upgrade are an afternoon.
- **Short sessions win on a phone.** A map should fit in about five
  minutes. If a run needs twenty, save and continue stops being optional.
- **Write the note while it is fresh.** Three lines after a run beat a page
  a week later.

## Pitfalls

1. **Polishing before proving.** Seven stages for four towers is 28
   sprites, more with animation. Do not start until the level 1 picks have
   survived the phone and the game has passed a few honest runs.
2. **The image model as a slot machine.** It will not hold a character
   steady across stages by itself. Three tries on a picture, then switch to
   the fallback: keep it as reference and have it redrawn in code. That is
   how today's best art was made, so it is not a defeat.
3. **Charm lost in cleanup.** Snapping a picture to the grid and palette
   can flatten what you liked about it. Judge the cleaned sprite in the
   game, not the original picture.
4. **Scope creeping in sideways.** Season palettes, twelve maps, the space
   theme, a story. Each is fine later. Each one added now pushes the first
   finished map further away.
5. **Number soup.** The plaque now carries six numbers. A cozy game should
   not need a spreadsheet. If you catch yourself ignoring the numbers,
   say so: fewer may be better.
6. **Reopening settled things out of boredom.** Reopen a decision when a
   run gives you a reason, not when a session needs something to do.
7. **Planning instead of playing.** This document is the last planning
   document the project needs for a while. The next useful thing is a run.
8. **Balancing for yourself.** After fifty runs the game will feel easy to
   you and be brutal for a friend. Use the simulator and other people.
9. **Two changes in flight.** One file, one change. An open pull request
   plus new edits is the classic way to lose an evening.

## Signs it is working

- You start a run when you do not have to.
- After losing you know what you would try differently.
- A friend finishes map 1 without help and asks for another go.
- You notice an upgrade happen without reading a number.

## What I am unsure about

- How hard the game is for a person. The script cannot say.
- Whether image-model art will import cleanly at this size. Untested on
  real pictures.
- Whether 28 tower sprites is affordable. Three stages per tower (base and
  the two forks, as today) with levels shown by a small added detail may be
  enough. Worth deciding after the first full tower.
